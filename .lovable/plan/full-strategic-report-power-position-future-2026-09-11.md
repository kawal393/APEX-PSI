# Full Strategic Report — Power, Position, Future

A single private document written to `/mnt/documents`, delivered in chat only. Nothing from this goes on the public site (private/strategy material is banned there).

## What the report will cover

**1. What actually exists today (verified, not claimed)**
Every live capability, checked against the code and the database before it is written down: the seal engine and predicate set, the verifier, the receipt format, the Bitcoin record and its real block height, the visit ledger, the founding registry, the API, the agent connector, and the watermarking work. Each item carries its true maturity label — live, reference, experimental, or planned — so the document survives hostile reading.

**2. Where the real power sits**
An honest account of what gives the position strength: the schema and receipt format as the thing others must adopt, verification being free and offline forever, the record surviving the company disappearing, the standards drafts, the patent, and first-mover timing against the current enforcement calendar. Each one rated for how durable it actually is.

**3. Where the position is weak**
The uncomfortable half: no packages published anywhere, no independent implementers, no outside node operators, no named adopters, single-operator dependence, one funding source, and the parts of the story that only hold because nobody has pushed on them yet. Named plainly, because a strategy built on a flattering audit fails.

**4. What would make the position genuinely hard to displace**
Concrete, ordered by leverage and cost, not by novelty:
- Getting the verifier into places where it becomes a default rather than a choice
- The receipt travelling inside things that already move (headers, files, feeds) instead of needing a visit
- Independent parties able to reproduce the record without asking
- Evidence that survives a cryptography change, and evidence that survives twenty years
- The path from "one operator" to "several, none of them required"

**5. The technology roadmap, in order**
What to build next and why each step raises the switching cost, from near-term work through to the multi-year items. Including where the current cryptographic choices will need to move, and the honest cost of each.

**6. Money**
Where revenue can come from without reintroducing public pricing or checkout, given commerce stays withdrawn: operational agreements, conformance work, retention and archive services, and institutional arrangements. What would need to be true for each to start.

**7. Twelve-month sequence**
A dated plan with the few things that matter most, and the tests that would tell us it worked.

**8. What could kill it**
The real risks: a competing standard arriving with distribution, a regulator naming someone else, a mathematical break, key loss, funding running out, and a claim that gets challenged publicly. With the response to each.

## Verification before writing

Before the report asserts anything about the current state, it gets checked: predicate counts read from the code, the Bitcoin block and confirmations read from the live records, package existence checked against the registries, ledger counts read from the live tables, and the routes read from the app. No number goes in unverified.

## Output

A long, dense document at `/mnt/documents/APEX_PSI_POWER_AND_FUTURE_2026-09-11.md`, plus a PDF version, both attached in chat for download. Every page of the PDF is checked visually before delivery. No website changes.
