        Binding Decisions and Settlements to Agent Action Records
                     draft-singh-psi-agent-02

Abstract

   This document extends [I-D.psi-agent-01], which records what a
   software agent did.  A record of an action does not show who said the
   work was to be done, what the model asked to do, what a policy engine
   permitted, or what was paid.

   This document defines four statements -- a mandate, a proposal, a
   decision, and a settlement -- and the rules that bind them to a chain
   of action records under one run identifier.  Each statement is signed
   by its own party with its own key.  A verifier that holds the
   statements can test, offline and without trusting the agent, whether
   the action chain is unbroken, whether the decision that permitted it
   was signed by a key other than the one named as the acting key, and
   whether the amount settled lay inside the limit the mandate declared.

   A valid envelope shows that identified parties made these statements
   and that the statements have not changed since they were sealed.  It
   does not show that any statement was correct, sufficient, lawful, or
   true, and it makes no legal representation of any kind.

1.  Introduction

   [I-D.psi-agent-01] seals a hash chain of agent actions.  That answers
   one question: was this recorded action altered, or omitted from the
   chain.  It does not answer the four questions an examiner of a
   disputed run asks first.  Who said this work was to be done, and
   within what limits.  What did the model ask to do.  What did a policy
   engine permit.  What was paid.

   Those four answers are normally produced by four different parties on
   four different systems, and none of them is in a position to sign the
   others.  This document gives each a statement of its own, sealed on
   its own, and defines the checks that join them.

   This document is a format and a verification procedure.  It is not a
   runtime, a permission system, a policy language, a payment protocol,
   an identity authority, or a legal rule.  It assumes a decision engine
   that the agent runtime cannot write to and cannot invoke on the
   agent's behalf; that assumption is a deployment property and this
   profile cannot verify it.

   The status of this document is Experimental as defined in [RFC2026].

1.1.  Scope of the novelty claim

   Action logs, signed allow or deny receipts, mandate and delegation
   credentials, Merkle transparency logs, and machine payment rails each
   exist separately as at the date of this document.  What this document
   specifies, and states no prior document known to the authors as at 6
   November 2026 specifies, is a settlement statement that a verifier
   checks against the spend limit declared in a separately signed
   mandate, bound to the same run as the action chain it paid for.
   Removing that check removes the reason for this document to exist;
   the remaining statements are conventional and are specified here only
   so that the check has inputs.

1.2.  Relationship to [I-D.psi-agent-01]

   This document does not redefine the action record.  It extends it,
   and Section 3.5 lists every extension and every new rule it imposes.
   Where this document and [I-D.psi-agent-01] differ on the action
   record, this document applies.

2.  Conventions and Terminology

2.1.  Requirements Language

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
   capitals, as shown here.

2.2.  Terminology

   Run:  a bounded sequence of actions taken by one software agent
      against one declared objective.
   Run identifier:  an opaque identifier, chosen before any statement is
      written, shared by every statement of one run.
   Statement:  one of the four objects defined in Section 3, or the
      envelope of Section 5.  A statement is sealed by the party that
      issues it and carries that party's signature.
   Action record:  the record defined in [I-D.psi-agent-01].  It is
      not a statement: it carries no signature of its own, and its
      digest is the chain digest of [I-D.psi-agent-01].
   Signed bytes:  the byte sequence defined in Section 4.1.
   Seal:  a signature over the signed bytes, together with the
      identifier of the key that produced it.
   Digest reference:  the string "sha256:" followed by the sixty-four
      lowercase hexadecimal digits of a SHA-256 digest.
   Envelope:  the object of Section 5.
   Issuer:  the party that seals a statement.
   Verifier:  a party that performs Section 6.

3.  Statements

   Four statements are defined.  A statement that a run does not contain
   is recorded in the envelope by the literal string "absent".  An empty
   object, an empty string, and omission of the envelope field all mean
   "malformed", not "absent".

   Every statement carries: ver, the profile version, which is the
   string "1"; the run identifier; its own identifier field; alg; kid;
   and sig.

3.1.  Mandate

   {
     "ver": "1",
     "mid": <digest reference>,
     "run": <run identifier>,
     "principal": <string, who declared the work>,
     "purpose": <string, free text, MAY be a commitment>,
     "scope": [<string, tool, host, or dataset name>],
     "limits": {
       "max_actions": <decimal string>,
       "max_spend": <decimal string>,
       "currencies": [<string>]
     },
     "not_before": <RFC3339 date-time>,
     "not_after": <RFC3339 date-time>,
     "alg": <string>,
     "kid": <string>,
     "sig": <base64url>
   }

   ver, mid, run, principal, alg, kid and sig are MUST.  purpose, scope,
   limits, not_before and not_after are SHOULD.  A mandate records that
   a key attributed to a principal signed this declaration.  It does not
   show that the principal was entitled to make it.

3.2.  Proposal

   {
     "ver": "1",
     "pid": <digest reference>,
     "run": <run identifier>,
     "mid": <digest reference of the mandate>,
     "proposer": <string, model or planner identifier>,
     "intent": <string, one line, no secrets and no personal data>,
     "args_digest": <digest reference>,
     "alg": <string>,
     "kid": <string>,
     "sig": <base64url>
   }

   ver, pid, run, mid, proposer, intent, args_digest, alg, kid and sig
   are MUST.  A proposal is what was asked for, before permission.  It
   is not evidence that anything was executed.

3.3.  Decision

   {
     "ver": "1",
     "did": <digest reference>,
     "run": <run identifier>,
     "pid": <digest reference of the proposal decided>,
     "verdict": "allow" | "deny" | "require_human",
     "policy_digest": <digest reference of the policy in force>,
     "decided_by": <string, engine identifier>,
     "reason": <string>,
     "alg": <string>,
     "kid": <string>,
     "sig": <base64url>
   }

   ver, did, run, pid, verdict, policy_digest, decided_by, alg, kid and
   sig are MUST.  reason is SHOULD.  The key named by kid is the key of
   the decision authority.  Section 6.6 tests that it is not the key
   named as the acting key of the actions the decision permits.

   "require_human" records that the policy engine referred the proposal
   to a person.  It does not record that the person approved it.  A
   deployment that wants to record the human outcome records it as a
   further decision.

3.4.  Settlement

   A settlement is a statement that a payment was made for this run.
   The party that signs it SHOULD be the payment provider rather than
   the agent, because the agent knows only what it asked for.

   {
     "ver": "1",
     "sid": <digest reference>,
     "run": <run identifier>,
     "did": <digest reference of the decision, or "absent">,
     "payer": <opaque string>,
     "payee": <opaque string>,
     "amount": <decimal string>,
     "asset": <string>,
     "status": "initiated" | "authorised" | "captured" | "settled"
             | "failed" | "refunded" | "reversed" | "disputed",
     "ref": <string, the provider's own transaction reference>,
     "scheme": <string, the payment scheme that produced ref>,
     "observed_at": <RFC3339 date-time>,
     "alg": <string>,
     "kid": <string>,
     "sig": <base64url>
   }

   ver, sid, run, payer, payee, amount, asset, status, alg, kid and sig
   are MUST.  did, ref, scheme and observed_at are SHOULD.

   These statuses are different claims and MUST NOT be treated as one.
   "authorised" records that a limit was reserved.  It does not record
   that money moved, and a verifier MUST NOT report it as meaning so.

3.5.  The action record

   The action record is the object defined in [I-D.psi-agent-01].  This
   profile makes four changes to it and no others.

   a.  The field prev is REQUIRED on every record of a run except the
       first, whose prev is the digest reference whose digest is sixty-
       four zero digits.  [I-D.psi-agent-01] makes prev optional and
       does not fix the first value.

   b.  The field did is REQUIRED on every record.  It holds the digest
       reference of the decision the action was taken under.

   c.  The field kid is REQUIRED on every record.  It names the key of
       the party that executed the action.  [I-D.psi-agent-01] does not
       define this field.

   d.  The field at is REQUIRED on every record.  It holds the RFC3339
       date-time at which the executing party reports the action.

   The kid of an action record names the key of the party that executed
   the action.  The record is sealed by the issuer that holds it per
   [I-D.psi-agent-01], and not by the named key.  The name is therefore
   attested by the sealing party and is not a signature by the party it
   names.  Section 6 compares names, and the comparison is only as
   strong as that attestation.

   The chain digest of an action record remains as defined in
   [I-D.psi-agent-01]: SHA-256 over the canonical form of the record.

4.  Canonical form, algorithms, keys and numbers

4.1.  Signed bytes and identifiers

   The signed bytes of a statement are the canonical form, per
   [RFC8785], of the statement with two members removed: sig, and the
   statement's own identifier member, which is mid, pid, did, sid or eid
   as applicable.

   The identifier of a statement is the digest reference of its signed
   bytes.  The alg and kid members are present in the statement and are
   covered by the signature.  A statement whose alg or kid is altered
   after signing MUST fail verification.

   The signed bytes of an envelope exclude a third member, anchor.  The
   anchor is evidence about the envelope and is not part of what the
   envelope asserts, so including it would make the envelope identifier
   depend on a tree that contains the envelope identifier.

   A signature is computed over the signed bytes.  The value of sig is
   base64url without padding [RFC4648].

4.2.  alg

   Every sealed object names its algorithm.  "ed25519" MUST be
   implemented [RFC8032].  "ml-dsa-65" MAY be implemented [FIPS204].  An
   unknown alg value is not a defect in the statement: a verifier
   reports algorithm_unavailable for that object, does not attempt to
   verify its signature, and reports the result of its other checks
   separately.

4.3.  kid

   kid names the key.  Its form is outside this profile.  A verifier
   resolves it by whatever means its deployment provides.  Statements
   are distinguished by the value of kid and not by a description of
   who signed.

4.4.  Dates

   Dates are RFC3339 date-times with an explicit offset [RFC3339].
   Precision to the second is sufficient.  A verifier MUST accept
   fractional seconds and MUST NOT require them.

4.5.  Decimal strings and assets
   A decimal string is an optional minus sign, then digits, then at most
   one full stop, then more digits.  No exponent, no floating point
   value, no leading or trailing space.  Comparison of two decimal
   strings is by integer part first and then by fractional part padded
   to equal length.

   asset and the entries of limits.currencies are compared as exact
   case-sensitive strings.  asset is an ISO 4217 alphabetic code where
   one exists and an upper-case ticker otherwise.

4.6.  Alternative transports

   A deployment MAY instead attach an HTTP Message Signature [RFC9421]
   to the transport that carries a statement.  That signature attests to
   the message and not to the statement.  A verifier that holds only the
   statement MUST NOT rely on it.

5.  The envelope

   {
     "ver": "1",
     "eid": <digest reference>,
     "run": <run identifier>,
     "mandate": <digest reference | "absent">,
     "proposal": <digest reference | "absent">,
     "decision": <digest reference | "absent">,
     "action_root": <digest reference | "absent">,
     "settlement": <digest reference | "absent">,
     "issued": <RFC3339 date-time>,
     "anchor": {
       "root": <hex digest>,
       "index": <integer>,
       "size": <integer>,
       "path": [{"side": "left" | "right", "hash": <hex digest>}]
     },
     "alg": <string>,
     "kid": <string>,
     "sig": <base64url>
   }

   ver, eid, run, mandate, proposal, decision, action_root, settlement,
   issued, alg, kid and sig are MUST.  anchor is MAY.

   The run identifier is assigned before any statement is written and is
   never derived from anything sealed later.  It is an opaque string
   with at least 128 bits of unpredictability, encoded in lowercase
   hexadecimal.  A value that a verifier could compute from the
   statements is not a run identifier.  The run identifier is not an
   authentication credential and MUST NOT be treated as proof of
   authority.

   action_root names the head of the chain: the record whose digest no
   other record of the run carries as its prev.  A run MAY contain more
   than one chain.  An envelope names one chain; a verifier given
   records of another chain reports them as an additional chain and not
   as an error.  Where the run has no action chain, action_root is the
   literal string "absent" and no record is walked.

   The envelope is itself a sealed statement, sealed by the party that
   assembles the run.

   One run has one current envelope.  When a settlement arrives after an
   envelope was issued, the holder issues a second envelope whose
   "replaces" member carries the eid of the first.  Both verify, and the
   later issued time wins.  "replaces" is MAY and, when present, MUST
   be a digest reference.

   anchor commits this envelope to an append-only Merkle tree as
   specified in [RFC6962], so that a party that does not hold the other
   statements can still be shown that the envelope existed.  The leaf
   of the tree is the UTF-8 encoding of the envelope's eid.  The path
   is the inclusion path of that leaf at index within a tree of size
   entries.  A verifier that is given an anchor MUST check the path
   against the root, and MUST check the root against a tree head it
   trusts.

6.  Verification

   A verifier is given the envelope and any subset of the statements.
   It performs the steps below.  Each step yields one result.  The
   result vocabulary is: ok, absent, invalid, algorithm_unavailable,
   key_unresolved, not_anchored.  A step that fails yields invalid and a
   reason code.  The reason codes used below are id_mismatch,
   envelope_ref_mismatch, held_but_envelope_says_absent,
   chain_broken, run_mismatch, decision_pid_mismatch,
   proposal_mid_mismatch, action_did_mismatch, denied_but_executed,
   decision_key_is_action_key, mandate_key_is_decision_key,
   mandate_key_is_action_key, currency_not_permitted, over_spend,
   over_actions, outside_window, inclusion_failed, signature_invalid.

6.1.  Statements

   1.  For each statement held, recompute the signed bytes and the
       digest reference, and compare the digest reference with the
       identifier member of the statement.  Fail with id_mismatch.

   2.  Compare the identifier member of each statement held with the
       corresponding member of the envelope.  Fail with
       envelope_ref_mismatch.  Where the envelope names "absent" and
       the verifier holds the statement, fail with
       held_but_envelope_says_absent.

   3.  Verify sig over the signed bytes under alg and kid.  Where the
       verifier does not implement the alg, report algorithm_unavailable
       and do not verify the signature.  Where the verifier cannot
       resolve the kid, report key_unresolved.  Otherwise, where the
       signature does not verify, fail with signature_invalid.

6.2.  The envelope

   4.  Recompute the signed bytes of the envelope and compare the
       digest reference with eid.  Fail with id_mismatch.

6.3.  The chain

   5.  Where the envelope names action_root "absent", there is no chain
       to walk and no record is walked, and the result is absent.
       Otherwise, starting from the record whose digest reference is
       action_root, follow prev to the record whose prev is the digest
       reference whose digest is sixty-four zero digits.  The digest of
       each record walked MUST be the digest reference that the next
       record names in prev, and the digest of the head record MUST be
       action_root.  Fail with chain_broken if no such record is
       reached, if a record repeats, if the chain does not terminate, or
       if a record walked lacks a field required by Section 3.5.
       Holding only the head is not verification of the run.

6.4.  Bindings

   6.  Every statement held MUST carry the run identifier of the
       envelope.  Fail with run_mismatch.

   7.  The pid of the decision MUST equal the pid of the proposal.
       Fail with decision_pid_mismatch.

   8.  The mid of the proposal MUST equal the mid of the mandate.  Fail
       with proposal_mid_mismatch.

   9.  The did of every record walked MUST equal the decision named by
       the envelope.  Fail with action_did_mismatch.

   10.  Where the decision is held and its verdict is "deny", the
       envelope MUST name action_root "absent" and MUST name the
       settlement "absent".  Fail with denied_but_executed.

6.5.  Keys, money and time

   11. The kid of the decision MUST differ from the kid of every record
       walked.  Fail with decision_key_is_action_key.  The kid of the
       mandate MUST differ from the kid of the decision and from the kid
       of every record walked.  Fail with mandate_key_is_decision_key
       or mandate_key_is_action_key.  Where a deployment permits two
       roles to share a key, the verifier reports the pair as a
       warning naming both roles.  This compares names.  The kid of a
       statement is signed by the party that issued it; the kid of an
       action record is attested by the party that sealed it, as
       Section 3.5 says.

   12. Where the settlement and the mandate are both held and the
       mandate declares limits.max_spend and limits.currencies: the
       asset of the settlement MUST appear in currencies, and the amount
       MUST NOT exceed max_spend.  Fail with currency_not_permitted or
       over_spend.

   13. Where the mandate declares limits.max_actions, the number of
       records walked MUST NOT exceed it.  Fail with over_actions.

   14. Where the mandate declares not_before and not_after, the field
       at of every record walked MUST fall inside that window.  Fail
       with outside_window.  These are times reported by the party that
       executed the action, and Section 8 says what that is worth.

6.6.  The anchor

   15. Where the envelope carries an anchor, verify the inclusion path
       against the root and the root against a tree head the verifier
       trusts.  Fail with inclusion_failed.  Where no anchor is present
       the result is not_anchored.

6.7.  Reporting

   A statement named in the envelope but not provided is reported
   absent.  A statement reported absent is not reported false.  The
   verifier reports the subset it held, and reports each step.

   The checks on keys, money and time are the reason this document
   exists.  A verifier that skips them has verified a bundle, not a run.

7.  Privacy

   The purpose and scope fields of a mandate, and the reason field of a
   decision, carry personal and confidential data.  For a field of low
   entropy the issuer SHOULD record a commitment instead of the value:
   the digest of the value concatenated with a salt of at least 128
   bits, the salt held by the principal.

   Digests permit correlation across runs that share a run identifier.
   An envelope is disclosable in more settings than the statements it
   names, not all of them.

   Statements MUST NOT contain secrets, credentials, or payment card
   data.

8.  Security Considerations

   A runtime that is compromised, or an honest runtime whose parties
   are colluding, can produce an envelope that satisfies every step of
   Section 6 for a run that did not happen as recorded.  This profile
   detects alteration of, and unrecorded omission from, the records it
   was given.  It does not detect a record that was never written, and
   it does not detect a set of statements all signed by parties acting
   together.

   Three things outside this profile carry the weight a reader may
   place on the format.  An anchor shows that an envelope existed in a
   tree the verifier already trusts.  Witnessing by parties outside the
   runtime, per [I-D.psi-agent-01], is the only check against
   collusion.  The independence of the decision engine is a deployment
   property, restated here because a separate key is not by itself a
   separate operator.

   An envelope records what its issuer knew.  A settlement that occurred
   and was not included is not detectable by this profile.

   The ref field of a settlement describes an external transaction and
   can be reused to describe a payment made for a different run.
   Section 6 checks it against the mandate, not against the payment
   system.  Where the scheme named by scheme permits a third party to
   confirm ref, a verifier that cares about the payment confirms it and
   says whether it did.

   Compromise of a signing key lets a party issue statements after the
   fact under an identity that verifiers trust.  Deployments MUST
   define rotation and revocation, and a verifier checking historical
   material MUST record what key status it assumed at what time.

   A digest of low-entropy content can be reversed by guessing.
   Section 7 requires salted commitments for that reason.

9.  IANA Considerations

   This document requests no IANA actions.  The enumerated values it
   defines are closed within this profile: the verdict values, the
   settlement status values, the alg values, and the statement types.  A
   profile that extends one of them carries a different value of ver.
   A verifier MUST report an unknown enumerated value as invalid and
   MUST NOT guess its meaning.
